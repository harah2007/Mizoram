    Scheduled Tribe & Scheduled Caste

    Mizoram's social structure is organised around tribe and clan, not the caste categories used in much of the rest of India, so a generic caste framework has not been applied here. A specific Scheduled Caste population figure could not be independently verified; the non-ST share shown alongside is a broader residual category (Scheduled Caste plus other non-tribal residents) and should not be read as an SC count.

    Reading ethnicity in Mizoram

    Four distinct classifications are often conflated in casual reporting on Mizoram: the official census category (e.g. Scheduled Tribe), ethnic identity (Mizo/Zo, Chakma, Tripuri, Bru/Reang and others), the underlying tribe or clan (Lusei, Hmar, Lai, Mara, Paite and dozens of others, many now subsumed under a shared Mizo identity), and linguistic identity (Mizo/Duhlian as lingua franca versus a household's first language). This dashboard keeps those four separate rather than treating them as interchangeable.

    Language

    Official & widely spoken languages

    Mizo (Duhlian) is the lingua franca; English is co-official

    Language, ethnicity and identity

    Mizo (based on the Duhlian/Lushai dialect) became the lingua franca across Mizoram's many tribal groups through 20th-century political consolidation, especially the Mizo Union's push to rename the Lushai Hills the "Mizo District" in 1954. English and Mizo are Mizoram's official languages; Hindi, Bengali, Chakma and other Tibeto-Burman languages are also spoken, particularly among the Chakma, Tripuri and Bru/Reang communities.

    Sources & methodology

    How this dashboard defines, sources and dates its figures.

    Reference years

    Demographic figures are drawn from the 2011 Census of India — the most recent decennial census conducted; the census originally scheduled for 2021 had not been conducted as of this dashboard's data cut-off. Where a more recent official estimate or survey (e.g. NFHS-5, 2019–21; state budget documents) is available, it is shown alongside the 2011 figure and labelled with its own year — the two are not blended.

    Administrative geography

    Mizoram has had 11 districts since 3 June 2019, when Hnahthial, Khawzawl and Saitual were carved out of Lunglei, Champhai and Aizawl districts. District-level 2011 Census figures shown here use the pre-2019, 8-district configuration, because that is what the census actually tabulated; retrospective population splits for the three newer districts vary between secondary sources and are not shown as a single authoritative figure. Map polygons are likewise drawn from an open dataset digitised to the 2011 configuration; the 3 newest districts are shown as labelled points rather than invented polygons.

    Party colours

    Colours used for political parties are chosen for legibility and consistency across charts and do not represent any party's official branding.

    What "Data unavailable" means

    Where a figure is marked unavailable, it is because no authoritative public source could be located or reconciled at the time of writing — not because the underlying reality is zero. This dashboard does not interpolate, model or estimate figures to fill gaps.

    Fact, interpretation and inference

    Historical and political narrative sections distinguish, where relevant, between documented fact, the interpretation of named historians or officials, and this dashboard's own analytical framing. Contested claims are flagged as contested rather than resolved.
